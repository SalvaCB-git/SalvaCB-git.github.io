Line-following robot

The wheel decision in this lab is the C function robot_select_wheels from robot_control.c, in the firmware of a university group project. It is compiled to WebAssembly unchanged; the sensors, the motors and the track are simulated in JavaScript, and nothing here was measured on the robot.

Drag the obstacle along the line, or move it with the slider, to change the ultrasonic distance. The function sets both wheel outputs to off at 20 cm or less; otherwise each infrared sensor drives one wheel.

The firmware does not say which sensor level means the line, or which wheel is which. The simulated infrared sensors output 1 over the floor and 0 over the line, and wheel1 is taken as the right wheel and wheel2 as the left; with those two assumptions the rule follows a line.

With nothing in its beam, the simulated ultrasonic sensor reports 400 cm and the car keeps driving. The firmware reads a missing echo as 0 cm, which the function treats as an obstacle and stops both wheels, so the real robot would behave differently there.

One simulated step is one decision; the real task decides roughly every 60 ms or more. The car's size and speed are not the robot's.
